One lab sheet, six constructions

The same order as the lab handout. Each value on the sheet comes from marks you placed, so you can check every step.

  1. Scale

    Click both ends of the scale bar and type its length. The second click snaps level with the first, so the measurement stays exact.

    m/px = L ÷ |x₂ − x₁|

  2. Intersection points

    Place a marker wherever a contact crosses a contour line. The marker takes the contour's elevation and the bed's colour.

    P = (x, y, h)

  3. Structure contours

    Points of one bed at one elevation are joined by a best-fit straight line. You see its length, how far each point sits off it, and where it crosses X–Y.

    min Σ dᵢ²

  4. Strike and dip

    Strike is the bearing of a structure contour. Dip comes from the spacing between two contours of the same bed, and points towards the lower one.

    tan δ = Δh ÷ d

  5. Cross-section

    Set X and Y. The section is drawn under the map at the map's scale; its height follows the points on the line. Change the vertical exaggeration if the handout asks for it.

    tan α = tan δ · sin β

  6. Table and legend

    Stratigraphic column and legend templates come filled in with your beds and colours. Edit them to match each assignment.

    young ↑ old ↓

Four tabs, one project

The workspace keeps the map, the section, the stereonet and the tables in step. Change a bed's colour once and it changes everywhere.

  1. A

    Map

    Load the map, set the scale, mark points and fit structure contours, one colour per bed. Lengths and positions on X–Y appear as you draw.

  2. B

    Cross-section

    Built from the section line automatically, at the map's scale. Apparent dips, antiform and synform hinges are labelled for you to interpret.

  3. C

    Stereonet

    Enter strike and dip, plot planes and poles, and find the line two beds share. Switch that line on to see it on the map.

  4. D

    Tables and legends

    Stratigraphic table and legend templates you can edit for each task, then export with the map and section.

Questions

  1. Which maps can I use?

    Any image of a map with contour lines and a scale bar: a scan, a photo of the lab sheet, or a screenshot of the PDF. Keep the photo square-on so distances are not distorted. Strikeline assumes north is at the top.

  2. Is my map uploaded anywhere?

    No. The map and everything you draw stay in your browser. Save the project as a file if you want to continue on another computer.

  3. Will it do the lab for me?

    No. You choose which points belong to which bed and what the structures mean. Strikeline does the arithmetic, keeps lines straight and scales exact, and shows how each number was found.

  4. My points don't fall on one straight line. What now?

    The best-fit line goes through them anyway, and each point shows how far it sits off the line. A point far off usually means a wrong elevation or a point on the wrong contact.

  5. Can I read it in Russian?

    Yes. Switch EN / RU at the top of any page. The terms follow the English lab handouts, so the translation helps with meaning without changing the vocabulary you hand in.

Compilation note

Made at NU by students. We built Strikeline for students and the geology department at Nazarbayev University after one too many evenings with a ruler, a protractor and a smudged lab sheet.

  • You do the geology. The tool measures and calculates. Choosing points, beds and interpretations is your work.
  • Every number has a source. Each length, angle and position links back to the marks it came from.
  • Your work stays yours. No accounts and no uploads. Projects are saved in your browser and as files.
